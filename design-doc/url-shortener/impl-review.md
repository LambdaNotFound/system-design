The simplest correct logic: (1) `canonicalize_v1` converts only a non-ASCII host through UTS #46 and percent-encodes other non-ASCII as UTF-8, then validates scheme (any case), RFC 3986 grammar and 2,048 characters on the converted form, then normalizes (lowercase scheme and host, uppercase hex, decode unreserved escapes including in the host, RFC 3986 5.2.4 dot-segment removal, drop `:80`/`:443` and an empty port, empty path to `/`), rejects any `@` in the authority and a host equal to or under `s.example` after one trailing dot is stripped, and hashes the canonical string with SHA-256 checked against published vectors. Codes are 8 base62 characters drawn without modulo bias from an injected source. (3) The resolver answers cache (entries younger than T, Live or Disabled only, never absent), then a local read bounded by S, then a consistent read capped at F in flight; absent from both is 404 and never cached, an error at step 4 is 503 and never falls back to stale data, and single-flight lets one fill per code while a failed leader releases its waiters.
